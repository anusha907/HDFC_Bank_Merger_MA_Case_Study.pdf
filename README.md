# HDFC Ltd – HDFC Bank Merger: M&A Case Study

An end-to-end analysis of the HDFC Ltd–HDFC Bank merger, India's largest M&A deal (~$48 billion), covering strategic rationale, valuation, synergies, post-merger performance, and risks.

**Full deck:** [HDFC_Bank_Merger_MA_Case_Study.pdf](./HDFC_Bank_Merger_MA_Case_Study.pdf)

## Deal snapshot
| | |
|---|---|
| Type | Reverse merger (HDFC Ltd into HDFC Bank) |
| Announced / Completed | 4 April 2022 / 1 July 2023 |
| Swap ratio | 42 HDFC Bank shares for every 25 HDFC Ltd shares |
| Result | Universal bank offering home loans and banking under one roof |

## What the analysis covers
- **Strategic rationale:** RBI's scale-based regulation, access to low-cost deposits, cross-selling, removal of the holding-company discount.
- **Valuation (3 methods):** comparable-company P/B (HDFC Ltd at 3.8x vs peers at 1.5–2x), DCF at ~13% cost of equity, and swap-ratio validation against independent valuers (Deloitte, S.R. Batliboi). Ranges overlapped around ₹2,200–2,500 per share, with the swap ratio implying ~₹2,400.
- **Synergies:** cost (~100–150 bps lower funding cost on the mortgage book, branch consolidation) and revenue (cross-selling to 68 million bank customers).
- **Promise vs reality:** NIM fell from ~4.1% to ~3.4–3.6% after the merger as rising rates repriced deposits faster than legacy loans. Integration and scale benefits largely delivered.
- **Risks:** margin pressure, asset quality, CRR/SLR requirements, integration, investor perception.

## Verdict
A strategically sound deal facing short-term margin headwinds. If margin pressure persists beyond FY26 without improvement, it would point to structural issues beyond interest-rate timing.
